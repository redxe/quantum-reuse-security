\section{Case Study I: Coherent Teleportation Cleans Two Qubits}
\label{sec:case-study-i}

Canonical quantum teleportation transfers an unknown state using entanglement,
two measurements, two classical bits, and conditional Pauli corrections
\cite{bennett1993teleporting,nielsen2010quantum}. The deferred-measurement
principle allows those measurements to be postponed and the classically
controlled corrections to be represented coherently
\cite{gurevich2022deferred}.

\subsection{Circuit}

Let $S$ be the source, $A$ an ancilla, and $B$ the destination. The circuit in
\cref{fig:teleport-cleanup} prepares a Bell pair on $AB$, performs the usual
Bell-basis transform on $SA$, replaces feed-forward by controlled $X$ and $Z$,
and finally maps $\ket{++}_{SA}$ to $\ket{00}_{SA}$.

\subsection{Derivation}

Let
\begin{equation}
  \ket{\psi}=\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1},
  \qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1.
\end{equation}
After Bell-pair preparation and the Bell-basis transform, the familiar
teleportation identity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \ket{\Psi_3}=\frac{1}{2}\big(&
  \ket{00}_{SA}\ket{\psi}_B
  +\ket{01}_{SA}X\ket{\psi}_B\\
  &+\ket{10}_{SA}Z\ket{\psi}_B
  +\ket{11}_{SA}XZ\ket{\psi}_B\big).
  \label{eq:teleport-branches}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The coherent $\cnot_{A\rightarrow B}$ removes the $X$ correction in the
branches with $A=1$:
\begin{equation}
  \ket{\Psi_4}=\frac{1}{2}
  (\ket{00}\ket{\psi}+\ket{01}\ket{\psi}
  +\ket{10}Z\ket{\psi}+\ket{11}Z\ket{\psi}).
\end{equation}
The coherent $\cz_{S,B}$ removes the remaining $Z$ correction when $S=1$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \ket{\Psi_5}
  &=\frac{1}{2}(\ket{00}+\ket{01}+\ket{10}+\ket{11})_{SA}
    \otimes\ket{\psi}_B\\
  &=\ket{+}_S\ket{+}_A\ket{\psi}_B.
  \label{eq:plus-plus-psi}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Finally, $H\ket{+}=\ket{0}$.

\begin{theorem}[Coherent teleportation cleanup]
For every single-qubit state $\ket{\psi}$,
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{clean}}\ket{\psi}_{S}\ket{0}_{A}\ket{0}_{B}
  =\ket{0}_{S}\ket{0}_{A}\ket{\psi}_{B}.
  \label{eq:cleanup-theorem}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:plus-plus-psi} factors the destination from the source and
ancilla. Applying $H_S H_A$ maps $\ket{++}_{SA}$ to $\ket{00}_{SA}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Entangled-reference preservation]\label{cor:reference-preservation}
Let $R$ be an arbitrary reference system and
$\ket{\Omega}_{RS}=\ket{r_0}_R\ket{0}_S+\ket{r_1}_R\ket{1}_S$.
Then
\begin{equation}
  (\id_R\otimes U_{\mathrm{clean}})
  \ket{\Omega}_{RS}\ket{00}_{AB}
  =\ket{00}_{SA}
   (\ket{r_0}_R\ket{0}_B+\ket{r_1}_R\ket{1}_B).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{eq:cleanup-theorem} to the $\ket{0}_S$ and $\ket{1}_S$ components
and use linearity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interpretation and resource tradeoff}

The result is a state-transfer identity on the initialized subspace
$\ket{\psi00}$. It is not a replacement for geographically distributed
teleportation: the coherent corrections require quantum interactions with the
destination. On a single processor, it must also be compared with a direct
SWAP, which may use fewer entangling operations.

Its value here is conceptual. The circuit has a formally provable postcondition:
two qubits are exactly known and separable. This is precisely the kind of fact a
resource-aware compiler could use. Existing qubit-reuse work commonly measures,
resets, and reallocates qubits \cite{decross2023reuse}. The circuit demonstrates
a coherent alternative: sometimes the program itself already uncomputes the
workspace into a reusable state.

\begin{claimbox}[Software cross-check]
The public artifact keeps a coherent cleanup implementation and a direct
SWAP-only state-transfer reference. Regression tests verify the same routed
output on the initialized subspace $\ket{\psi00}$ and verify preservation of an
entangled reference. This comparison is deliberately restricted: agreement on
$\ket{\psi00}$ does not imply that the complete unitaries are globally equal on
arbitrary three-qubit inputs.
\end{claimbox}
